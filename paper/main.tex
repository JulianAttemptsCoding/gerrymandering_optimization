\title{Certified Seat--Safety Frontiers for U.S.\ House Districting:\\ Counterexample-Guided Geographic Relaxation under County-Split Budgets}
\author{Draft}
\date{\today}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Heuristics can draw maps with many safe seats but cannot show that nothing better exists. We define the \emph{seat--safety frontier}, the largest number of U.S.\ House districts that can simultaneously clear a robust two-party margin $m$, and its dual, the \emph{spectrum} of best achievable $q$-th district margins, and certify both at voting-precinct resolution. Aggregated relaxations with exact-integer allocations, fractional-knapsack hull cuts and quotient-graph connectivity, refined by a counterexample-guided loop, end in an independently verified plan or an infeasibility proof; we prove validity, monotone refinement and exactness. Coarsening connectivity alone cannot beat the geography-free bound (provably, and on real precincts); a county-split budget is what makes coarse relaxations informative. For 16 states with two to five districts, both parties, $\pm1\%$ population balance and at most $k-1$ county splits, we bracket the spectrum within half a point in 44 of 104 cases and pin the number of districts reaching 50\%, 55\% and 60\% exactly in 79 of 96. Verified plans attain from 11 to 30 Democratic-majority districts of these states' 52 seats (2020 presidential vote; proofs exclude anything outside 11--36); both parties can draw a majority of the delegation in Iowa, Nevada and New Mexico, and in four states the delegation is the same for every plan. No enacted 118th-Congress plan reaches the certified capacity at any rank, and the eight that satisfy the regime pass as independent tests of the proofs. Every bound is a verified map or an exact-integer proof, audited against an independent MILP encoding and brute-force enumeration. The frontier is symmetric in the parties, forecasts nothing and carries no legal claim.
\end{abstract}
